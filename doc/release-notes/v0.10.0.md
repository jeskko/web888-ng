VHF reception (experimental): airband and 2 m through the Web-888's VHF
input, with the VHF channelizer bitstream of web888-ng-hw v0.5.0.

> **VHF still needs testing against real signals or a signal
> generator.** It has run on a Web-888 with nothing on its VHF
> connector: the clock, the bitstream, the RF switch and the frequency
> mapping work, but no VHF station has been received with it yet.
> Levels, sensitivity and audio quality on real signals are unknown.

* **`[radio] band = "vhf"`** (or `--band vhf`), zynqsdr backend:
  * the ADC clock (Si5351) at 98.304 MHz before the bitstream loads, the
    RF switch on the VHF input, and 98.304-147.456 MHz tunable
    (undersampled: the ADC's third Nyquist zone). The web UI, the Kiwi
    protocol and HPSDR see the real frequencies;
  * `bitstream = "channelizer"` takes the image's channelizer for the
    band (`websdr_pfb_vhf.bit` on VHF), `auto` its tuned bitstream
    (`websdr_vhf.bit`, 13 receivers; the same frequency mapping);
  * web888d refuses a channelizer built for another clock than the one
    programmed;
  * **back to HF** is a restart with `band = "hf"`: web888d notes the
    clock it set (in `/run/web888`) and sets 122.88 MHz again. Without
    that note an HF start leaves the clock alone, as before.
* **NBFM**: a new mode for FM voice (2 m). ±5 kHz at 12 kS/s, as a
  KiwiSDR does (5 kHz deviation's peaks are clipped; 2.5 kHz fits).
  The squelch for the noise between transmissions. Kiwi clients' `nbfm`
  now gets FM instead of AM.
* **Web UI**: an FM button (key f), 12.5 kHz steps in FM; the phone's
  band picker offers the bands in the receiver's range (Air 118-137 MHz
  in AM and 2 m in FM on VHF).
* **Skimmers**: bands outside the receiver's range aren't run (logged
  once). On VHF the HF bands' FT8 skimmers stop instead of decoding
  another frequency and reporting wrong spots.

On HF nothing changes except the NBFM mode and the skimmer range check.

Tested: unit tests (the frequency mapping, the bins, NBFM's level,
distortion and squelch), NBFM and AM end to end on a synthetic VHF
recording (IQ backend), and on a Web-888 (2026-11-12) with the VHF
connector open: the clock (RX at 12 035 frames/s), the bitstream's
checks, the RF switch (the HF antenna's stations more than 55 dB down),
zoomed views, and the way back to HF. That unit has run this version on
HF since then, with FT8 skimmers on ten bands.
