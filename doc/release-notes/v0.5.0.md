More listeners on one Web-888, audio over plain HTTP, and the first
release meant for web888-ng's SD card image (web888-ng-os):

* **Audio over plain HTTP.** Browsers allow the audio worklet only on
  HTTPS pages, so a unit opened as `http://<unit>:8073` played no sound.
  Such pages now play through a fallback on the page's own thread, with
  bigger blocks and a deeper buffer (about a quarter second more
  delay); the status bar says so and links to the same page over HTTPS
  (web888d's own certificate: the browser warns once). HTTPS works as
  before.
* **Load shedding, measured on a Web-888** (240 kHz views, FT8
  skimmers on all bands):
  * The DSP workers shed waterfall work when they fall behind on audio
    (more than half the RX queue waiting, or a block lost), and take it
    back once they have caught up. 16 listeners: clean audio, waterfalls
    at 17.5 lines/s (10-11 before). 32 listeners: clean audio once
    connected (a few gaps while 32 connect at the same moment), 5.9
    lines/s.
  * When the decoders keep running out of time, the band with the fewest
    recent decodes is skipped, one at a time, and resumed after two calm
    minutes (`/metrics`: `skim_shed`, `skim_slots_shed_total`).
  * New sessions are set up one per DSP round, with shared FFT plans.
* **FT8 a third cheaper** on the A9 (5.0 to 3.4 s per slot); a slot's
  time is shared by its band's cost when the decoders are short.
* **`[limits] trusted`**: addresses or networks (`192.0.2.0/24`) that
  the per-address session limit doesn't count, e.g. a club's own
  network. Applied on reload, no restart.
* **Pseudo-stereo in SSB** is panned by pitch on a log scale around the
  passband's middle, so voice spreads across both ears.
* With a channelizer bitstream (not released yet): waterfall views up to
  7.68 MHz from per-bin FFTs, read in bursts; dragged views keep their
  FFTs; wide views drawn interpolated.

With the stock-compatible bitstream (web888-ng-hw v0.2.0) web888d works
as in v0.4.0 apart from the above.
