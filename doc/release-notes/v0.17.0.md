The receiver as a clock: time-code stations worldwide decoded on the
receiver and handed to chrony, so the unit keeps time without the
Internet, and can serve it to the shack's computers over NTP. Details in
`doc/api.md` §8.5.

* **Time-code stations** (`[timecode] enabled`; needs the channelizer
  bitstream, the default): each minute's carrier, decode and lateness,
  the clock's offset by each station, as metrics, a log and the admin
  **Clock** tab.
  * `[timecode] region = "auto"` (default) picks the stations in reach
    of `[station] locator`; or `europe`, `north-america`, `east-asia`,
    `all`; `stations` names them outright.
  * LF: DCF77, MSF, RBU, TDF (Allouis, its phase-modulated code), WWVB,
    JJY (40 and 60 kHz), BPC, and the EFR teleswitch stations' time
    telegrams (DCF39, HGA22, DCF49).
  * HF (`centre_hz` on the carrier wanted): WWV/WWVH in AM, each minute
    told to be WWV's or WWVH's by its 1000 or 1200 Hz tones; BPM and RWM
    (ticks only: their minutes are named by the clock).
  * The path from each transmitter (ground wave on LF, sky-wave hops on
    HF) and the decoders' own delays are taken off. Measured on a
    Web-888 in northern Europe: RBU, DCF39, DCF77, MSF. Provisional, from
    GPS-timed KiwiSDRs: TDF, WWVB, JJY, BPC, BPM, RWM, WWV. WWVH isn't
    calibrated on its own.
* **To chrony** (`[timesync]`, needs web888-ng-os v0.14.0 or later):
  once a minute the stations' offsets combined (outliers out, weighted
  by their errors; a large offset only when two stations agree) to
  chrony's reference-clock socket. While NTP answers it leads; without
  the Internet the stations keep the clock, typically within a few
  tenths of a millisecond.
  * A station's error includes its calibration's uncertainty and jumps
    at dusk and dawn; minutes less sure than `max_sigma_ms` (0.5 ms) are
    pooled, up to `pool_minutes` (10), before chrony gets a sample. On
    two days of a unit's minutes this cut the worst samples (99th
    percentile) from 3.7 to 0.5 ms.
* **NTP for the local network** (Clock tab, `[timesync] serve_lan`, off
  by default): chrony answers devices on private and link-local
  addresses, so a station where the Internet is poor (a DXpedition) can
  take its time from the receiver. The switch spells out the risks
  first: everyone on a shared network can ask it, and port 123 must
  never be forwarded from the Internet.
* **Clock tab** (admin): chrony's tracking and sources, the kernel's
  clock, what goes to chrony (and what is pooled), the stations, the
  receiver's timebase, NTP for the LAN.
* **Time panel** (the receiver page's decoders): TDF, BPC, DCF39,
  HGA22, DCF49, BPM (AM) and RWM besides DCF77, MSF, WWVB, JJY, RBU and
  WWV/WWVH; minutes show which of WWV and WWVH sent them.
* Translations: the new texts in Finnish, Swedish and German.

Not yet: GPS as a time source (waits for testing on a unit with a GPS
antenna); WWVB's phase code; weak-signal (coherent) reception of BPC
and TDF.

Tested:
* Unit tests; the decoders on recordings from KiwiSDRs in Europe, North
  America, Hawaii, New Zealand, Japan and Taiwan and from a Web-888 in
  northern Europe, through both the monitor (`tcbench`) and the page's audio
  path (`replay`); the combination and pooling replayed on two days of a
  unit's minutes.
* On a Web-888 (the channelizer bitstream) since 2026-11-19:
  the LF monitor and chrony; a 24-hour run with the stations as chrony's
  only source (90 % of the time within 0.5 ms of NTP, dawn and dusk up
  to 5 ms before the pooling); NTP for the LAN opened and closed UDP
  123; this build since 2026-11-22.
