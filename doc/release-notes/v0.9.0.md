Zoomed waterfalls from the FPGA: with the channelizer bitstream of
web888-ng-hw v0.4.0, many listeners can each have their own zoomed view.

* **Zoom spectra from the FPGA's zoom engine** (bins mode,
  `doc/m6-channelizer.md` §6). A zoomed view used to run its own FFTs
  on the CPU, about 7-9 % of a core for each distinct view. Now the
  FPGA computes the spectra and web888d sums them.
  * On a Web-888, 16 listeners each with their own 480 kHz view run at
    20 lines a second without an audio gap, the DSP pool at 60 % of a
    core. Before, the pool was full and the views dropped to 17 lines a
    second, with gaps.
  * The view looks the same: its spectrum from the engine and from the
    CPU's FFTs agree within 0.2 dB.
  * It needs web888-ng-hw v0.4.0's `websdr_pfb.bit` and driver
    (web888-ng-os v0.6.0 has both). With an older bitstream or driver
    web888d runs the views' FFTs itself, as before.
  * `[radio] zoom_engine = false` (or `--no-zoom-engine`) turns it off.
* **Sharper wide views.** A 15.36 MHz view, and a 30.72 MHz view on a
  2048-point display, now come from the engine too: 15 kHz display bins
  where the power line gave 30 kHz ones stretched. The price: such a
  view costs 3 to 5 % of a core, and its noise floor is grainier (2 to
  3 dB from line to line; the power line's 0.3 dB). The full band and
  30.72 MHz on 1024 points draw the power line as before.
* `webload` (the load test tool): `WEBLOAD_DUMP=FILE` writes a session's
  averaged spectrum.

Without the channelizer (the HF bitstream, the stock driver, the IQ and
simulated backends) nothing changes, except that the IQ backend has a
software zoom engine for tests.

This version has run on a Web-888 since 2026-11-11 (the channelizer with
the zoom engine, FT8 skimmers on ten bands): load tests of 8 and 16
listeners, some hours in all.
