Signals explained and labelled, neural noise reduction in the page
(experimental), DGPS beacons, twin passband tuning. Everything new
runs in the listener's browser; the receiver's own load is unchanged.

* **"What is this?"** Shift-drag or shift-click on the waterfall (or a
  signal row's ?): the page listens for about 4 s and says what the
  signal is and why: a headline, a few sentences and the measured
  figures (shift, baud, bandwidth, sweeps). Measurements and known
  signatures are checked against the signal identifier, now 25 classes
  (Olivia/MFSK, DominoEX, Hell, PACTOR, MT63, the 39-tone modem, 3G
  ALE, DRM, sigidwiki.com's utility signals, radars and sounders by
  their sweeps), with a calibrated "unsure". DRM, broadcast AM and
  radars are looked at in the channel's IQ, ±10 kHz.
* **Signal labels** (Panels list, needs the channelizer bitstream): the
  identifier runs over the receiver's bins across the waterfall's view
  (up to 1 MHz) and names every signal on the spectrum.
* **Neural noise reduction, experimental** (the Noise box's **NN**,
  marked *exp.*): small models running in the browser, one per kind of
  signal, with a strength slider (30-300 %). They take noise away and
  never add sound. They are still being trained and scored, and later
  releases will change them.
  * SSB voice (USB/LSB): trained on speech through a ham transmitter's
    processing, simulated HF paths and real receiver noise.
  * CW: keeps the keyed tones and quietens the noise between and around
    them. It never re-keys.
  * AM broadcasts (speech and music), after SAM and the auto notch.
  * Needs 12 kHz audio.
* **Gen, experimental** (beside NN in SSB voice; fetched on first use,
  about 3 MB): a generative restorer that rebuilds the voice after the
  noise reduction. It sounds clearer, but it may now and then add sounds
  or words that weren't said; the decoders never hear it. The shipped
  weights are the best of seven training rounds (`gen5f3`). On 1000
  real KiwiSDR receptions (the Paderborn database) its speech
  intelligibility index (ESTOI) is 0.375, against 0.351 for NN alone
  and 0.341 for the receiver's own audio. Speech recognisers make more
  mistakes on its output, though: 60.7 % of words wrong with Whisper,
  against 52.5 % for NN alone.
* **DGPS** (decoders): maritime DGPS beacons, 283.5-325 kHz, MSK at
  50-200 Bd. Every beacon in the passband is found. Its RTCM SC-104
  messages are read: reference station, health, position (with the
  distance and bearing from `[station] locator`), each satellite's
  correction plotted over 15 minutes, texts and the almanac of other
  beacons. **Wide** skims the 40 kHz bin around the dial instead of the
  audio passband (needs the channelizer bitstream).
* **Twin passband tuning:** the passband button opens the presets, PBT 1
  and PBT 2 (lower and upper edge), Shift and Clear. A passband change
  is now faded in, without a gap or a click.
* **The page tidied:** Noise, WF, Rec, stream and Panels boxes instead of
  rows of buttons. The header stays on one row on a 14-inch laptop. The
  waterfall's auto range is damped.
* RTTY panel: slow, narrow FSK is listed by its per-bit SNR. For
  example, RDL on 18.1 kHz (50 Bd, 75 Hz shift) was hidden before.
* Time panel: TDF is shown as "ALS162 (TDF)", and `ALS162` is accepted
  in `[timecode] stations`.
* Translations: the new texts in Finnish, Swedish and German.

Tested:
* Unit tests; the decoders and the identifier on recordings (KiwiSDRs
  and a Web-888 in northern Europe); DGPS on 60 kS/s recordings of the
  beacon band (27 beacons, positions and almanacs consistent); the
  noise reduction models scored with Whisper and Parakeet on the
  Paderborn KiwiSDR database.
* On a Web-888 (the channelizer bitstream): each feature live since it
  was written (2026-11-23..28): NN and Gen on live SSB, CW and AM
  broadcasts; DGPS Wide (11 beacons from one dial); signal labels on
  40 m.

**Pre-release:** the release waits for tests on a second unit.
