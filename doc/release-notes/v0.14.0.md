More decoders in the browser (M8), and receiver controls: each listener's
page decodes CW, RTTY/NAVTEX/DSC, PSK, HF fax, SSTV, HFDL, JS8 and the
time stations itself, names the mode of a signal in the passband, can
record IQ, and has AM AGC speeds and 24 kS/s audio. The unit only sends
audio (or a channelizer bin), so the decoders cost it nothing. Plan and
test results: `doc/m8-browser-decoders.md`.

* **Decoders** (the **Decoders** button; each opens a panel beside the
  waterfall):
  * **CW**: the signal at the CW offset with its speed, and every other
    CW signal in the passband. There is also a neural decoder, trained
    on synthetic CW including pile-ups, fading and auroral flutter; it
    is on by default and can be switched off.
  * **RTTY / FSK**: the shift, baud rate, framing, polarity and
    character set worked out from the signal. Ham RTTY, DWD weather,
    SITOR-B and NAVTEX (messages listed by their B1-B4 header) decode
    with no settings. Weak characters are dimmed, not guessed.
  * **DSC** (ship and coast station calls, ITU-R M.493) in the RTTY
    panel: the call's type, who calls whom, the frequencies or the
    distress position, with its check sum. The six DSC distress and
    safety channels are in the station list.
  * **PSK31/63/125**: every BPSK signal in the passband.
  * **HF fax and SSTV**: weather charts and SSTV images (Martin,
    Scottie, Robot, PD), slant corrected, saved as PNG.
  * **HFDL** (aircraft data link): position reports, logons and ACARS,
    checked by their CRCs; aircraft and ground stations on the map.
  * **JS8**: all four speeds; senders' grids on the map.
  * **Time stations**: DCF77, MSF, WWVB, JJY (carrier keyed, in USB, LSB
    or CW) and WWV/WWVH (AM): the minute's bits as they come, then the
    time and date with the stations' checks, confirmed when two minutes
    agree, and how late they arrived by the server's clock.
* **Signal identifier**: names the mode of the signal in the passband
  (18 classes: RTTY, NAVTEX, fax, SSTV, PSK, FT8, CW, SSB, AM,
  STANAG 4285, MIL-STD-188-110, HFDL, OTH radar, interference, ...) and
  opens its decoder.
* **Wide skimming** (with the channelizer bitstream): the CW and RTTY
  panels can take a whole channelizer bin (60 kS/s, about 40 kHz)
  instead of the audio, and skim every signal in it.
* **IQ recording**: **Rec IQ** records the bin around the dial (60 kHz,
  16-bit) for 10 s to 5 min, in the browser, and saves it as .cs16 or
  .wav.
* **AGC speed in AM**: fast, medium (the default) and slow, as in SSB.
  Fast follows the carrier as before. In quick fading, medium and slow
  hold the gain through each fade instead of raising the noise.
* **24 kS/s audio**: beside the passband presets, 12 or 24 kS/s.
  12 kS/s stays the default; 24 kS/s takes the passband to ±10 kHz
  (broadcast AM, NBFM) and twice the data (48 kB/s). On the channelizer
  bitstream, whose receivers are 12 kS/s, the audio comes from the
  channelizer bin (at most 8 listeners at a time; others get 12 kS/s).
  Kiwi clients stay at 12 kS/s. API: `audio_rate` in `state` and
  `audio`, `audio_rates` in `hello`.
* The waterfall zooms with a trackpad's travel, not a fixed step per
  event; the decoders are under one button.

Tested:
* Unit tests.
* The decoders on recordings (`doc/m8-browser-decoders.md` §4 and
  each decoder's section): real CW, RTTY, NAVTEX, fax, HFDL, JS8 and
  DSC, and DCF77 from a Web-888.
* The panels in headless Chromium.
* On a Web-888 (the channelizer bitstream): this UI and server, with
  real DSC calls decoded in the page, IQ recordings checked sample by
  sample, and 24 kS/s audio. A 24 kS/s listener costs about 2 % of a
  core more than a 12 kS/s one.
* On synthetic signals only: SSTV, and MSF, WWVB, JJY and WWV's time
  codes. Fax was read from a recording (DWD, started by hand), NAVTEX
  from synthetic messages; neither has been watched live on a unit yet.
