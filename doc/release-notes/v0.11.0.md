FT8, FT4 and WSPR decoding in the browser, on the frequency you tune,
and skimmers that need about a third less of the Web-888's CPU: a unit
can now run FT8 and FT4 on all of 160-6 m at once.

* **FT8, FT4 and WSPR in the browser.** The **FT8/FT4/WSPR** button
  opens a list beside the waterfall (on the phone too): pick the mode
  and one of its usual dial frequencies, and each slot heard whole is
  decoded in the browser with the skimmers' own decoders (WebAssembly).
  * The decodes stay in the page: nothing goes to PSKReporter or
    WSPRnet, and the unit spends nothing on them.
  * **Deep** searches harder for a few more decodes (FT8: about 1 % more
    of what WSJT-X finds) for two to four times the CPU, still a few %
    of one core.
  * The slots follow the receiver's clock (taken from the waterfall),
    so a wrong clock on your computer doesn't matter.
* **Skimmers: less CPU for the same decodes.**
  * Candidates that can't be a signal are dropped earlier, and the
    search works on a decimated band with short FFTs. On the Web-888 a
    busy FT8 slot takes 2.3 s of CPU instead of 3.5 s, a quiet one 0.5 s
    instead of 0.8 s, a busy FT4 slot 1.0 s instead of 1.4 s.
  * The same decodes as v0.10.0 on our test recordings (two more on one
    30 m set), checked against WSJT-X on a second set of recordings
    covering 160-10 m.
  * The decoders' scheduling: an FT4 slot no longer gives up time to
    FT8 slots that are due later. With FT8 and FT4 bands together, FT4
    slots used to be cut short, and their bands shed, even with CPU to
    spare.
* **Fix:** picking a JTTY dial frequency on a band that defaults to LSB
  (40 m) left the receiver in LSB.

Tested: the decoders on recordings scored against WSJT-X (`jt9`, both
sets) and `wsprd`; the browser's decoder in Node on the same recordings
(the same decodes as on the server) and in Chromium against a replayed
recording; on a Web-888 (2026-11-12), FT8 on 11 bands and FT4 on 9
(160-6 m) for 12 minutes with every slot decoded and the CPU 21 % idle,
nobody listening (listeners come first: with many, the quietest bands
are shed as before), and the browser's FT8 decodes on 20 m against the
unit's own skimmer (86 messages in common, about 10 marginal ones on
either side). That unit runs this version.
