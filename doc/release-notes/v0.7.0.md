A phone layout, frequency labels with a band ribbon, and a new starting
view.

* **Phone layout** (contributed by j0uni, #1). On a narrow window, or a
  phone held sideways, the receiver gets a layout of its own:
  * the dial in large type at the top, with its own keypad for entry;
  * the waterfall filling the screen, with pinch to zoom;
  * a thumb dock with the modes, steps, passband and a band picker that
    remembers where you were on each band;
  * a VFO wheel (24 clicks a turn; its centre cycles the step);
  * everything else under More.

  `?ui=mobile` or `?ui=desktop` forces either layout. It has been used on
  an iPhone against a Web-888.
* **Frequency labels.** The Labels button (under More on a phone) shows
  labels along the top of the spectrum when the view is 3 MHz wide or
  narrower. Click a label to tune there in its mode; hover for its
  schedule and notes. Four sources:
  * **Broadcast:** EiBi's shortwave schedules (season B26), only what is
    on the air now;
  * **Utility:** time signals, maritime safety (DSC, NAVTEX, distress
    voice), VOLMET and other aeronautical frequencies, weather fax, DWD
    RTTY, the NCDXF beacons;
  * **Amateur:** the digital-mode dials (FT8, FT4, WSPR, JS8...) and the
    calling, QRP, SSTV and emergency frequencies of the IARU region;
  * **Station:** the receiver's own list, which its admin uploads and
    downloads as CSV in the admin pages' new Labels tab.
* **Band ribbon** between the spectrum and the scale:
  * the amateur band plan of the IARU region, in its CW, narrow-band
    digital and all-mode segments;
  * the broadcast, maritime, aeronautical, time-signal and ISM bands.

  Each listener can pick IARU region 1, 2 or 3. The receiver's default is
  `[markers] region`.
* All the label and band-plan data is part of web888d; nothing is fetched
  at runtime.
  * EiBi's schedule is used as published, with thanks to Eike Bierwirth
    (eibispace.de): free to use and distribute.
  * The rest is compiled from the IARU band plans, the ITU Radio
    Regulations and the stations' operators' own publications;
    `crates/markers/data/README.md` lists each source.
  * Each label also records its signal's kind (RTTY, fax, DSC, FT8...) and
    published parameters, for decoders to come.
* **New sessions start on 7.066 MHz LSB** with a 960 kHz view around the
  dial (the idea from #2, by jorksx), instead of the whole band.
  `[web] default_freq` still sets another frequency; LSB below 10 MHz,
  USB above.
* **Fixes:**
  * Tuning faster than the server answers no longer puts the dial back:
    an old answer to an earlier tune is recognised and ignored. This
    affected the desktop's digit wheel too.
  * A bad waterfall colour range can no longer blank the spectrum and
    waterfall. The floor and top sliders now keep at least 10 dB apart.

On a Web-888 with the channelizer bitstream, the 960 kHz starting view
comes from per-bin FFTs: about 17 % of one A9 core, shared by everyone on
that view. On the standard bitstream it is an ordinary waterfall slot.

This version has run on a Web-888 since 2026-11-04 (the channelizer
bitstream, FT8 skimmers on ten bands).
